Step One

Arrival & settling in

The house is at 3107-1 Hotaka, Kijimadaira. The Lawson convenience store is right across the street, so it's easy to find. We'll send you details for access, water heating and anything else you need to know before you arrive.

  • 01Parking. The driveway fits two cars. In winter there's a snow shovel in the shed. Clear the driveway after overnight snow, it's part of village life and will help with your muscles!!
  • 02The genkan (entrance room). Shoes off at the door, always (please). There's space for everyone's shoes.
  • 03Supplies. Lawson across the street covers the first night and satisfies most munchies. For a bigger shop, head to the big supermarket TSURUYA Iiyama (next to the shinkansen station).
  • 04Internet. House WiFi is coming soon, so pick up an eSIM before you travel.
Arriving by train? Shinkansen to Iiyama (about 1 hr 45 min from Tokyo), then a local bus or taxi for the last 15 minutes.
Entry & Gear

The genkan & drying room

Like most Japanese homes, the genkan marks where you leave the outside world. Outdoor shoes stay down below. Step up into the house in socks or house slippers.

The drying room is new. Hang up wet jackets, pants and gloves in the evening and everything is dry by morning. It sounds like a small thing but it makes a real difference.

  • 一Ski boots and wet snow gear go to the drying room. Not the genkan, never on tatami.
  • 二Hang things with space between so air gets through. Gloves dry fastest if you open them up.
  • 三Skis, boards, bikes and toboggans live in the storage shed with the snow shovel.
  • 四Slippers stay off the tatami. Tatami rooms are socks or bare feet only.
Day to Day

Living in the house

The bath

The soaking bath fills automatically. Use the shower station to wash first, then soak. The water stays in the bath and everyone uses it over the evening (or maybe just for the kids). That's the normal way in Japan. It's one of the best things about the house after a day outside. Alternatively stop off at an onsen on the way home.

Heating & the kotatsu

Reverse cycle aircon is being installed at the end of 2026. Additional winter heating runs on kerosene. The tank is in the shed and we keep it full. The heated kotatsu table in the lounge is where everyone ends up. Legs under the blanket, hands around something hot. Turn the heaters off when you go out.

Kitchen & laundry

The kitchen is fully equipped (albeit basic). Fridge, stove, everything you need to cook for the group. The washing machine means you can pack lighter, especially with the drying room.

Futons & sleeping

Beyond the four bedrooms there are four single futons. Lay them on any tatami room with the bedding provided. Fold them up and air them in the morning rather than leaving them down all day. Keeps them fresh.

Rubbish & recycling

Rubbish sorting matters in a small village. It's one of the main things the neighbours notice. The bins in the house are labelled. The main types are:

Burnable Food scraps, soiled paper, and most general waste
Plastics Rinsed plastic packaging and wrappers with the プラ mark
Cans & Glass Rinsed cans and bottles, sorted separately
PET Bottles Caps and labels off, bottle rinsed and crushed
When in doubt, rinse it and ask us — a quick message beats a wrong bin. Collection days and any house-specific notes are in your arrival details.
