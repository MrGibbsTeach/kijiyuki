Step One

Arrival & check-in

The house is at 3107-1 Hotaka, Kijimadaira — the Lawson convenience store is directly opposite, which makes it easy to spot. We'll send you entry details and anything stay-specific before you arrive.

  • 01Parking — the driveway fits two cars. In winter, a snow shovel lives in the shed; clearing the driveway after overnight snow is part of village life.
  • 02First stop, the genkan — shoes off at the entry, always. There's space for everyone's footwear (see below).
  • 03Supplies — Lawson opposite covers the first night; the local supermarket (イーストア滝沢商店) handles a proper shop.
  • 04Connectivity — house WiFi is coming soon, so pick up an eSIM before you travel. Mobile coverage in the village is good.
Arriving by train? Shinkansen to Iiyama (~1 hr 45 min from Tokyo), then a local bus or taxi for the last 15 minutes — or ride the iiyama Line one more stop to Kijimadaira station. See the iiyama Line guide.
Entry & Gear

The genkan & drying room

Like every Japanese home, the genkan (entryway) marks the line between outside and inside. Outdoor shoes stay on the lower level; step up into the house in socks or the house slippers.

The drying and mud room is brand new and is honestly the best thing about coming back from the snow — hang wet jackets, pants and gloves in the evening and everything is warm and dry by morning.

  • 一Ski boots, wet boots and snow gear go straight to the drying room — not the genkan, and never onto tatami
  • 二Hang gear with space between items so air can circulate; gloves and liners dry fastest opened up
  • 三Skis, boards, bikes and toboggans live in the storage shed, alongside the snow shovel
  • 四Slippers stay off the tatami — tatami rooms are socks or bare feet only
Day to Day

Living in the house

The bath

The deep soaking bath fills automatically and keeps the water hot. It works like an onsen at home: shower and wash first at the shower station, then soak. The bathwater stays clean and is shared by everyone over the evening, so it isn't drained between people — this is the normal Japanese way and one of the great pleasures of the house after a day outside.

Heating & the kotatsu

Winter heating runs on kerosene — the tank lives in the shed and we keep it topped up. The heated kotatsu table in the lounge is where everyone ends up; legs under the blanket, hands around something warm. Please switch heaters off when you head out for the day.

Kitchen & laundry

The kitchen is fully equipped — fridge, stove, and all the appliances you need to cook for a full house. The washing machine means you can pack lighter than you think, especially with the drying room next door.

Futons & sleeping

Beyond the four bedrooms there are four single futons that can be laid out in any tatami room. Lay them directly on the tatami with the provided bedding; fold and air them in the morning rather than leaving them down all day — it keeps both the futons and the tatami fresh.

Rubbish & recycling

Sorting rubbish properly matters a lot in a small village — it's the single most visible way guests can do right by the neighbours. The bins in the house are labelled; the main categories are:

🔥 Burnable Food scraps, soiled paper, and most general waste
♻️ Plastics Rinsed plastic packaging and wrappers with the プラ mark
🥫 Cans & Glass Rinsed cans and bottles, sorted separately
🧴 PET Bottles Caps and labels off, bottle rinsed and crushed
When in doubt, rinse it and ask us — a quick message beats a wrong bin. Collection days and any house-specific notes are in your arrival details.
Before You Travel

What to pack

The house covers more than you'd expect — bedding, kitchen, laundry, drying room, bikes and toboggans in the shed. Filter the list for the season you're visiting.

  • WinterYour own ski boots if you have them — rentals are fine, your own are better. Everything else hires easily at the resorts
  • WinterSnow-rated footwear for the village — pavements and the driveway are properly snowy
  • WinterWarm layers — thermals, fleece, beanie and gloves; the drying room means one good set goes a long way
  • WinterGoggles & sunscreen — bluebird days on snow are bright
  • GreenWalking or hiking shoes — trails range from boardwalks at Kayanotaira to proper hikes on the Shin-etsu Trail
  • GreenA light rain shell — mountain weather turns quickly, especially in June and July
  • GreenInsect repellent for forest walks and summer evenings
  • GreenSun protection — hat and sunscreen for cycling and open trail sections
  • GreenA day pack for hikes, bike rides, and trips on the iiyama Line
  • AlwaysAn eSIM or pocket WiFi — house WiFi is coming soon, so sort data before you fly
  • AlwaysCash (yen) — rural Japan still loves cash; smaller restaurants and onsens often don't take cards
  • AlwaysA small onsen towel — handy for the public baths at Nozawa; you can also buy one there as a souvenir
  • AlwaysA translation app downloaded for offline use — you'll get by comfortably, this just makes menus more fun
  • AlwaysSlippers or warm socks if you like your own — the house is socks-and-slippers territory
