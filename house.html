Step One

Arrival & check-in

The house is at 3107-1 Hotaka, Kijimadaira. The Lawson convenience store is right across the street, so it's easy to find. We'll send you keys and anything else you need to know before you arrive.

  • 01Parking. The driveway fits two cars. In winter there's a snow shovel in the shed. Clear the driveway after overnight snow, it's part of village life.
  • 02The genkan. Shoes off at the door, always. There's space for everyone's shoes below.
  • 03Supplies. Lawson across the street covers the first night. For a bigger shop, there's a local supermarket (イーストア滝沢商店).
  • 04Internet. House WiFi is coming soon, so pick up an eSIM before you travel. Mobile coverage in the village is fine.
Arriving by train? Shinkansen to Iiyama (about 1 hr 45 min from Tokyo), then a local bus or taxi for the last 15 minutes. Or take the iiyama Line one more stop to Kijimadaira station. See the iiyama Line guide.
Entry & Gear

The genkan & drying room

Like every Japanese home, the genkan marks where you leave the outside world. Outdoor shoes stay down below. Step up into the house in socks or house slippers.

The drying room is new. Hang up wet jackets, pants and gloves in the evening and everything is dry by morning. It sounds like a small thing but it makes a real difference.

  • 一Ski boots and wet snow gear go to the drying room. Not the genkan, never on tatami.
  • 二Hang things with space between so air gets through. Gloves dry fastest if you open them up.
  • 三Skis, boards, bikes and toboggans live in the storage shed with the snow shovel.
  • 四Slippers stay off the tatami. Tatami rooms are socks or bare feet only.
Day to Day

Living in the house

The bath

The soaking bath fills automatically. Use the shower station to wash first, then soak. The water stays in the bath and everyone uses it over the evening. That's the normal way in Japan. It's one of the best things about the house after a day outside.

Heating & the kotatsu

Winter heating runs on kerosene. The tank is in the shed and we keep it full. The heated kotatsu table in the lounge is where everyone ends up. Legs under the blanket, hands around something hot. Turn the heaters off when you go out.

Kitchen & laundry

The kitchen is fully equipped. Fridge, stove, everything you need to cook for the group. The washing machine means you can pack lighter, especially with the drying room.

Futons & sleeping

Beyond the four bedrooms there are four single futons. Lay them on any tatami room with the bedding provided. Fold them up and air them in the morning rather than leaving them down all day. Keeps them fresh.

A day at the house

There's no script here. People make their own rhythm. Breakfast might be cooking together in the kitchen or grabbing something quick from Lawson across the street. Then depending on the season: slopes or a hike, cycling on farm roads, visiting the snow monkeys, chasing cherry blossoms, exploring the area. In the afternoon, soak in the bath or head to a nearby onsen. Dinner is whatever you want — cook together at the house or head into town or over to Iiyama. Evening might be feet under the kotatsu, cards around the table, or quiet time in separate rooms. It's your week.

Rubbish & recycling

Rubbish sorting matters in a small village. It's one of the main things the neighbours notice. The bins in the house are labelled. The main types are:

🔥 Burnable Food scraps, soiled paper, and most general waste
♻️ Plastics Rinsed plastic packaging and wrappers with the プラ mark
🥫 Cans & Glass Rinsed cans and bottles, sorted separately
🧴 PET Bottles Caps and labels off, bottle rinsed and crushed
When in doubt, rinse it and ask us — a quick message beats a wrong bin. Collection days and any house-specific notes are in your arrival details.
